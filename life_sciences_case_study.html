Key Learning

We learned that conversational AI must match its response to customer intent and perceived risk, knowing when to guide, when to act, and when to simply provide data.

Context

In life sciences AI is changing the customer expectation from "show me where to go" to "help me get this done."

Problem / Opportunity

Our customers are forced to think in terms of which products do what rather than what they're trying to accomplish, because data, workflows, and capabilities are fragmented across disconnected products, requiring them to repeatedly relearn where to go and how to adapt their workflows to different UX.

Contribution

Led the platform-wide AI UX strategy from vision through execution, unifying the MC suite across products, data, and workflows through a single entry point while consolidating a dozen single-purpose AI experiences into one cohesive orchestration layer. See my process

2nd Order Impact

Built the framework and design system to scale AI across siloed products, shifting from isolated features to a unified system.

Applied UX Laws*

Hick's Law

The time it takes to make a decision increases with the number and complexity of choices.

Tesler's Law

The Law of Conservation of Complexity, states that for any system there is a certain amount of complexity which cannot be reduced.
